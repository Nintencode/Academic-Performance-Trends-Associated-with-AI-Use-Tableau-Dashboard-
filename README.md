# Academic-Performance-Trends-Associated-with-AI-Use-Tableau-Dashboard-
This visualization utilizes the ai_student_impact_dataset available on the Tableau Public Platform. The purpose of this dashboard was to explore the data and find trends in how AI usage could affect student's performance.

The visualization has 4 panels, and it is interactive. You can click on different sections such as the students majors, AI primary usage,  student's year (freshmen, sophomore, junior, and senior) and more for a comprehensive view. 


### The 4 panels display the following: 

**- Top Panel: Data Overview -** This highlights key KPIs, such as average GPAs pre and post semester, the average GPA change by percentage and whole value, average weekly AI usage hours, and number of students.

**- Middle Panel: GPA Change by Student Majors and Their Main Purpose of Using AI -** This showcases the average change in GPA by major for each Primary AI Use Case. As noticed throughout the visualization, AI usage leads to a positive change in GPAs across the board.

**- Bottom Left Panel: Average GPA Change for Traditional Study Hours (AI Hours Color Coded) -** This scatter plot illustrates the relationship between traditional study hours and GPA change, with point color representing weekly AI study hours. A positive trend is observed between traditional study time and GPA improvement. AI usage is distributed across all GPA outcomes, suggesting no clear independent relationship between AI hours and GPA change. Not shown is a scatter plot I created to see the relationship between GPA change and AI usage, the trend was primarily flat indicating very steady slow growth than compared to traditional hours. Overall, the results indicate that traditional studying remains strongly associated with improved academic performance, while AI appears to be most effective as a complementary study tool.

**- Bottom Right Panel: Percentage of Student's Overall GPA Trend -** This panel shows the overall trend of whether a student's GPA increased or decreased following the semester of AI usage. The results show overwhelmingly that AI can be a useful tool in the classroom when used effectively to enhance learning capabilities.

### Conclusion:
Overall, this dashboard suggests that AI can be an effective academic tool when used appropriately to support learning. Across the dataset, most students experienced GPA improvements following the semester, with positive trends observed across majors and AI use cases. These findings suggest AI is most beneficial when used to enhance, rather than replace, traditional learning.
